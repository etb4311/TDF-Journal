### 09.08.26: Role card from a real conversation

### 09.03.26–09.08.26: v1 baseline and tuning against my own answers

## 2. System Diagram: How It Works

*This diagram shows how the system functions (inputs, processing, decisions, outputs), not what the interface looks like.*

<!-- TODO: export slide 5 of the deck as a PNG to Week4/assets/09-18/slide-05.png, then uncomment -->
<!-- ![Reliable Peer system diagram](assets/09-18/slide-05.png) -->

**Walkthrough of the diagram:**
1. **Input:** The student fills in an initial ask, desired output, symptoms/reason, logistics, and one of four tone modes.
2. **Processing:** An Express server (`server.ts`, `/api` endpoint) assembles the prompt from those inputs plus the role card constraints.
3. **Decision / logic:** The role card (9-field system instructions plus a knowledge base covering Hugh Dubberly, the MDes program, Tang Center policy, and Jacobs Hall studio culture) drives a four-stage loop: suggest → diagnose → clarify → offer alternative. Gemini (`@google/genai`) returns structured output for each stage.
4. **Output:** Peer guidance, a drafted letter, a policy check, and one-click edits.
5. **Feedback loop (if any):** The student adjusts the draft with one-click edits. A rule-compliant fallback advisor runs when no API key is present, so the guardrails hold offline.

## 3. System Card Versions

| Version | Date | What changed | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| v0.1 | 09.08.26 | Initial card: nine-field role card written from an observed conversation | Turn the behavior I already performed into a spec |
| v0.2 | 09.03–09.08.26 | Five added rules, stochastic raised to 2.0, knowledge base attached | v1 was too sympathetic, too diagnostic, and too granular against my real replies |
| v0.3 | 09.18.26 | Same role card moved into `MDES_AGENT.md`; scope widened from letter writing to all coursework and code; knowledge base swapped to MDes, journals, and toolchain | The rules describe how I want AI to treat my thinking, not how to write a letter |

### Reliable Peer system card (v0.2)
- **Purpose:** Help classmates with school questions. Informational, not conversational.
- **Intended users:** MDes classmates dealing with absences, medical policy, and studio logistics.
- **How it works:** A four-stage loop (suggest → diagnose → clarify → offer alternative). Behavioral rules: high-level, short, single paragraph, casual tone, first names in the body, lead with empathy, phrase advice as "I would…", logistics only after acknowledgement.
- **Known limitations:** Still sounds like software. Rules get over-interpreted (empathy first even when asked to just write the message). The form-driven flow is not conversational, and the agent's reasoning is not visible behind the GUI.
- **Risks & mitigations:** Does not fabricate, does not over-sympathize, does not dump detail (all in the "does not do" field). The policy check and fallback advisor keep guardrails in place. TODO: risk of giving wrong medical-policy information.

### Why v0.3: the letter writer was a first draft of a broader assistant

Reliable Peer started as a letter writer, but the role card underneath it made no claims about letters. Its purpose was "augment, don't replace," its loop was suggest → diagnose → clarify → offer an alternative, and its rules were about tone and restraint. The only letter-specific parts were the tone modes and the knowledge base. That made the role card portable, and my own assistant setup was the obvious place to test it.

In v0.3 the same nine fields govern the assistant I work with day to day, and the full text lives in the agent repo: [etb4311/TDF-Agents](https://github.com/etb4311/TDF-Agents) (`MDES_AGENT.md`, "Role card" section). The repo is private, so the mapping below quotes the relevant lines.

| Role card field | Reliable Peer (v0.2) | General assistant (v0.3, `MDES_AGENT.md`) |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Help classmates with school questions; informational, not conversational | Augment my thinking, never replace it; a peer, not a therapist or ghostwriter |
| Interaction loop | suggest → diagnose → clarify → offer alternative | Same loop, layered on the existing plan-first workflow ("suggest" is the plan, "clarify" is the question before a big step) |
| Behavioral rules | Short, casual, empathy first, "I would…" | Same, and explicitly applied to code: a failing build gets one line of empathy before the fix |
| Does not do | No over-sympathizing, fabrication, follow-up questions, or detail dumps | Same, plus no silently taking over decisions of taste or academic judgment |
| Knowledge base | Dubberly, MDes, Tang Center, Jacobs Hall | Dubberly, MDes courses, studio culture, my journals, and my toolchain |
| Known failure mode | v2 front-loaded empathy even when asked to "just write the message" | Written into the card: when I ask for a plain deliverable, deliver first |

What stayed the same: the feeling and the logic. What changed: the surface. Instead of a form and four tone modes, the assistant applies the rules in conversation, which is also the fix I proposed for Reliable Peer's biggest weakness (it felt like software rather than a friend). It carries the same risk, too: rules that are too eager get over-interpreted, so that lesson from v2 is now a line in the card.
