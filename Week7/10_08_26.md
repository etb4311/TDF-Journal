## 2. Process Documentation

### Setting up the starter
- **What I did:** Created a fresh Unity 6000.6.3f1 URP project, cloned the instructor's DigiPhant starter next to it, and imported the rigged elephant and the reference scripts. Built a Python environment for the camera tracker.
- **Why:** The starter supplies the elephant, a MediaPipe pose tracker and a working reference scene. The assignment is to adapt it, not rebuild it.
- **What happened / what I learned:** The Mac only had Python 3.9, which is too old for MediaPipe 0.10.35, so I installed Python 3.14 through Homebrew. All 124 supplied asset fingerprints matched and the starter's 11 Python tests passed before I changed anything, so I had a known-good baseline.
- **Next step:** Get the camera talking to Unity.

### Failed output: the camera that opened and closed
- **What I did:** Pressed Play. The webcam light flashed on and straight off, and the Console said `Camera stopped delivering frames.`
- **Why it happened:** I tested both cameras the Mac could see. Camera 0 failed its first two frame reads and then worked; camera 1 worked from the first frame. The tracker treats a single failed read as fatal, so it quit immediately.
- **What happened / what I learned:** The second "camera" was my iPhone, connected through Continuity Camera. Using camera 1 worked as a stopgap; turning Continuity Camera off fixed it properly. I chose not to add code to the tracker for this, because the real fix was the setup, not the software. The hardest bug of the day wasn't in any code.
- **Next step:** Build the scene the elephant will perform in.

### Savannah course and camera view
- **What I did:** Ran the instructor's tool that injects a savannah obstacle course into the scene. It made its own backup first. Then I tried locking the camera behind the elephant for a third-person view.
- **What happened / what I learned:** All of the course's validation checks passed, but the tool warned that the elephant could only travel 15 units from its start and the course needs about 30. I raised it to 50 in the Inspector. Parenting the camera to the elephant gives a locked third-person view with no code.
- **Next step:** Decide who controls what.

### Drift: faking physics
- **What I did:** Asked whether the elephant could drift like a car, and whether that needs a physics engine.
- **What happened / what I learned:** It doesn't. The elephant's movement script places it directly every frame, so a drift can be faked by letting the direction of travel lag behind the direction the elephant faces. That's also how most arcade racing games do it. A loose grip reads as sliding; a tight grip reads as normal walking.

```csharp
// PhoneDrift.cs: grip decides how fast the travel direction catches up with the facing
float grip = Drifting ? driftGrip : normalGrip;
velocity = Vector3.Lerp(velocity, (moved - last) / dt, 1 - Mathf.Exp(-grip * dt));
Vector3 next = last + velocity * dt;
```

### Building the three-person scheme
- **What I did:** Turned the notebook control matrix (section 3) into code. Added two MediaPipe models to the tracker: a Gesture Recognizer for fist and peace sign, and an Object Detector for a phone. Wrote Unity scripts that play the elephant's eat and drink animations and the phone drift.
- **Why:** MediaPipe's pose model only sees where wrists are, not what the fingers are doing, so hand shapes and objects each needed their own model.
- **What happened / what I learned:** A code review caught a bug where eat replayed by itself if tracking flickered for one frame. It now needs the gesture gone for 0.3 s before it can fire again.

### Failed output: nobody gets assigned
- **What I did:** Tested with two of us while our third teammate was away.
- **What happened / what I learned:** With the group size set to 3, neither of us was assigned. It isn't a bug: the tracker waits until it sees as many people as the group size before numbering anyone, so people walking into frame don't get the wrong player number. The workaround for rehearsing is a stand-in (a photo of a person on a screen) or switching to 2 people while testing. The system won't start until the whole group is there, and that turned out to be a design statement in its own right.

### Failed output: "Packet isn't the sole owner of the holder"
- **What I did:** The tracker crashed inside MediaPipe's gesture model while several hands were in view.
- **What happened / what I learned:** I couldn't reproduce it with Google's sample images, which only ever showed one hand. Another team's working version looked for 2 hands per person while ours looked for 8, so I limited ours to 2 per person. The tracker now also restarts the gesture model on that error instead of taking pose tracking down with it.

My notebook sketch had three columns: who controls it, what the elephant does, and which gesture triggers it. This is what it became in code:

| Person | Elephant motion | Gesture | How it's read |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Forward, backward, steering | Arms and shoulders | Pose landmarks → hand motors |
| P2 | Eat | Fist, held 0.3 s | Gesture Recognizer, `Closed_Fist` |
| P2 | Drink | Peace sign, held 0.3 s | Gesture Recognizer, `Victory` |
| P3 | Drift | Phone held up | Object Detector, "cell phone", near P3's wrist |

The sketch left P3 open between "speed, drift or spin". We picked drift because it makes a two-person move: P3 decides when the elephant loses grip, and P1 decides which way it slides.
