Write three paragraphs about your goals at MDes that you would be happy to share with a classmate, could be your application personal statement

At Northwestern, I realized my passion for design is driven by its potential to help everyday people solve complex problems. At the Segal Design Institute, I developed a strong technical foundation in engineering while participating in projects with direct human impact. The shop and design studios became second homes for me; here I was able to operate CNC machines for mold design, sketch tiny home designs, and utilize lab space to determine the best method for thawing meat. Alongside coursework in mechanical design, manufacturing, and prototyping, I actively sought creative leadership opportunities. I enrolled in high-level design-focused courses, directed the a cappella group Asterik, conducted design research for professors, and contributed to graduate research through lab work in the Mechanical Engineering and Astronomy Departments. These experiences taught me to approach problems systematically, balancing technical feasibility with user needs, narrative clarity, and collaboration. This combination of rigor and creativity continues to define my approach as a designer.

After graduating during the pandemic, I entered industry to apply my skills. At Tesla, I worked as a Mechanical Design Engineer on Model 3 and Model Y control panels, developing production-ready solutions, coordinating logistics for new product introductions, and rapidly iterating prototypes under tight time and budget constraints. I cherished the opportunity to get literal hands-on experience prototyping and retrofitting such a well-engineered product. Riding around the factory track for noise-vibrational harshness testing was another great bonus. While I valued the dynamic environment, I felt disconnected from the human consequences of the mechanical design decisions. That led me to Arktura, an architectural manufacturing company in Los Angeles. Working as a Product Development Engineer, I assisted operators, identified process bottlenecks, and learned  to operate machinery such as press brakes and Zünd cutters to prototype independently. I led the establishment of a UV printer workstation, developed testing protocols, and managed print systems for R&D. I quickly discovered a gap between human needs, work habits, and technological solutions. This clarified my desire to focus on design as a means of aligning complex technological systems with the people who use them.

These experiences have brought me to an inflection point. To grow beyond an engineer and into a designer capable of shaping complex socially embedded systems, I seek deeper instruction, collaboration, and opportunity. I yearn to go beyond executing plans on the factory floor. I want to generate human-centered design plans that consider multiple perspectives and consequences. Graduate study will provide the technical and collaborative refinement to extend my mastery as a designer.
